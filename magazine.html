When Mitt Romney Said
Corporations Were People,
I Felt That

I was working in branding at the time — Wolff Olins, where we spent our days treating companies like living things with identities, personalities, and developmental arcs. So when Romney made his remark, it didn't sound absurd to me. It sounded like an accidental admission of something the branding world had been acting on for decades: that companies behave like characters. That they have birth dates, formative traumas, peak moments, and declines.

The question that opened this zine wasn't do corporations have charts. It was: what happens when you look? What do you actually find when you take the birth date of Apple Computer — April 1, 1976, a garage in Cupertino — and run it against the sky?

You find Aries. You find a company that arrived like a declaration. Then you run the incorporation date — January 3, 1977 — and you find Capricorn: structure, institution, ambition harnessed. Then the IPO — December 12, 1980 — and you find Sagittarius: the myth goes public, the story becomes an offer to the world.

Three births. Three charts. Three different faces of the same company. I don't know if the cosmos arranged Apple. I know that when you map it this way, something comes into focus that doesn't come into focus any other way. That's enough for a zine.

This is Issue 01. It's about Apple. The next issues are about companies you probably use every day. Each one was born somewhere under some configuration of sky. We're just going to start looking.

— Astrology Collide  ·  Vol. I, Issue 01

01 Feature
The Three Births

Three Births,
Three Charts

Most companies have one founding date. Apple has three — and each one tells a different story about what the company is. Not versions of the same story. Different stories. Which means Apple has always been plural. It has always been more than one thing at once.

♈

April 1, 1976

The Soul

Aries Sun · The First Fire

April Fool's Day. Jobs chose the date — some accounts suggest deliberately. Aries is the sign of pure initiation: the first fire, the cardinal act, the leap before the map exists. There is no chart more appropriate for a company that began with Wozniak showing Jobs a circuit board in a garage and Jobs saying: this changes everything.

The Aries Sun gives Apple its irreducible conviction. It is always, at its core, an act of declaration. Every product launch, however polished, still contains this original gesture: something willed into existence by sheer Aries nerve.

♑

January 3, 1977

The Body

Capricorn Sun · The Institution

Apple Computer Inc. is legally born under Capricorn: the architect, the mountain goat, the sign that builds structures meant to last centuries. This is the Apple that hired Mike Markkula, raised venture capital, drafted shareholder agreements, and learned to think in quarters. The Capricorn chart is the machine behind the dream.

Notice what Capricorn brings that Aries lacks: patience, institutional memory, and the ability to operate at scale. Every time Apple makes a decision that prioritises long-term positioning over short-term flash, it is operating from this chart.

♐

December 12, 1980

The Market Persona

Sagittarius Sun · The Myth Made Public

The IPO. The day Apple became a thing anyone could own a piece of. Sagittarius is the sign of philosophy, far horizons, and the arrow shot toward a truth not yet visible. This chart rules Apple's public mythology: its think different campaigns, its keynotes as sermons, its products as invitations to a better version of your life.

The Jupiter return to 1° Taurus on the founding date — and Uranus's return to 2° Taurus on August 2, 2018, the day Apple crossed $1 trillion — is not something the company planned. But it is something the sky recorded.

02 Feature
The Founders as Archetypes

Four Men, Four Archetypes

Every company at its origin is a cast of characters playing roles that the mythology later simplifies. Apple had four founders. Most people remember one. But each brought something the others couldn't — and each embodies a different relationship to the work of building something new.

🌊

The Magician · Tarot I

Steve Jobs

Sun Pisces · Moon Aries · Mercury Aquarius Rx

The Pisces Sun gives Jobs his peculiar quality — the ability to see what doesn't exist yet and treat it as already real. Pisces is the final sign, the sign that dissolves boundaries, the sign of the visionary who cannot always distinguish between the world as it is and the world as it could be. In Jobs, this was not a flaw. It was the mechanism.

His Aries Moon is the urgency beneath the vision — the emotional need for initiation, for motion, for the constant arrival of the new. The famous reality distortion field is not a trick. It is the natural output of a Pisces Sun convinced of what it sees, operating through an Aries Moon that cannot tolerate stillness.

Mercury retrograde in Aquarius: Jobs' mind was unconventional even by unconventional standards. He thought backwards from a future most people couldn't picture. Mercury Rx suggests a processing style that internalises before it speaks — which explains both his silences and his pronouncements.

⚡

The Inventor · Tarot XVII

Steve Wozniak

Sun Aquarius · The Engineer of the Impossible

Wozniak is the Aquarian ideal made flesh: the technologist as humanitarian, the inventor who built the Apple I as a gift for friends and was surprised when Jobs saw a product in it. The Apple I and Apple II were Wozniak's machines — technically brilliant, built for joy, almost entirely uninterested in commerce.

Aquarius is the sign of the future arriving early. Wozniak arrived early. He was doing in 1975 what the industry would spend the next decade learning to do. He didn't need the mythology that Jobs built around the work — the work was its own mythology to him.

The partnership of Jobs and Wozniak is an Aries/Aquarius alliance: fire and air, the visionary and the inventor, the salesman and the engineer. Neither could have built what they built without the other, and both knew it, and that knowledge didn't always make things easy.

🚶

The One Who Walked Away · Tarot 0

Ronald Wayne

The Third Founder · Sold His Stake for $800

Ronald Wayne is the third founder nobody talks about. He drew the original Apple logo — Newton under the apple tree — and wrote the first partnership agreement. Twelve days after signing, he sold his 10% stake back to Jobs and Wozniak for $800. At Apple's 2023 valuation, that stake would have been worth approximately $300 billion.

Wayne later said he had no regrets. He had his reasons — he was older, had other debts, and assessed the risk differently than two men in their twenties with nothing to lose. His is the archetype of the Fool at the beginning of the tarot: the one who stands at the edge, sees the cliff, and steps back.

The Wayne story is the shadow story of every founding. Not every character who appears at the origin survives to the mythology. His exit is as much a part of Apple's chart as any planet.

♍

The Administrator · Tarot VIII

Tim Cook

Sun Scorpio · The Virgo Era Made Flesh

Jobs handed the company to Cook on August 24, 2011 — a Virgo date, a Virgo transfer. Cook is the archetype of Strength, the tarot card of inner mastery, of power held rather than performed. Where Jobs operated from Pisces dissolution and Aries fire, Cook operates from precision, process, and supply chain as art form.

The Cook era is marked by Apple's move from products as mythology to products as infrastructure. The iPhone under Cook became the most profitable product in history. Under Cook, Apple crossed $1 trillion (August 2, 2018) and $3 trillion (January 2022). The sky on August 2, 2018 showed Uranus at 2° Taurus — nearly conjunct Jupiter at 1° Taurus from Apple's founding date.

Scorpio Sun, Virgo transition date, trillion-dollar scale. The administrator who made the myth into infrastructure.

03 Feature
The Product Rituals

Products as Rituals

Every Apple product launch happens on a specific date, in a specific sky. Over forty years, a pattern emerges: certain signs appear at pivotal moments, Jupiter and Saturn mark the transitions, and the launches that changed everything tend to cluster around cardinal points and sign-change boundaries.

January 24, 1984

☉ Aquarius

The Macintosh

Aquarius · The Machine That Speaks to the People

"Hello." The Mac introduced itself. No product had done that before, and Aquarius — the sign of the future, the collective, the democratisation of intelligence — is exactly the right sky for a machine that claimed to put the power of a computer into the hands of everyone. The 1984 ad aired once during the Super Bowl and changed advertising permanently. That is an Aquarius move: the single gesture that rewrites the rules.

October 23, 2001

☉ Scorpio

The iPod

Scorpio · 1,000 Songs in Your Pocket

Scorpio governs depth, obsession, and the intimate. The iPod arrived six weeks after 9/11, in a culture suddenly desperate for private experience, for the ability to disappear inside music. "One thousand songs in your pocket" is a Scorpio promise: not a feature list, an interior world. The device that made Apple cool again launched under the sign of interiority.

June 29, 2007

☉ Cancer

The iPhone

Cancer · The Phone That Became Home

Cancer rules home, belonging, the body as a nest. The iPhone launched under Cancer and became, within five years, the place where most people lived their emotional lives: their photos, their relationships, their memories. Jobs' own Jupiter and Uranus sit in Cancer in the 10th house — public career, world-changing work. The iPhone is the most literal expression of his natal chart's promise.

April 3, 2010

☉ Aries

The iPad

Aries · The Post-PC Beginning

Jobs called it the most important thing he'd ever done. He launched it under Aries — the founding sign, the sign of first gestures. The iPad arrived as a declaration that the personal computer era was ending and something else was beginning. Aries again, doing what it always does: showing up at the origin point and naming what comes next.

February 2, 2024

☉ Aquarius

Apple Vision Pro

Aquarius Again · Spatial Computing Arrives

Apple returns to Aquarius for its most radical product since the iPhone. Vision Pro is the company's first spatial computing device — the beginning, Apple says, of a new era. Aquarius appeared at the Mac in 1984. It appears here again, forty years later, for the next paradigm. The sign of the future-that-arrived-early, repeating.

"The iPhone launched under Cancer — the sign of home, belonging, and the body as a nest. Within five years, it had become the place where most people lived their emotional lives."
— Astrology Collide, Issue 01

04 Feature
The Store as Temple
🏛️ Fifth Avenue · New York · 2006

Retail as Sacred Architecture

Four Temples
and a Glass Cube

The first Apple Store opened May 19, 2001 — Taurus season, the sign of beautiful objects, of value made tangible, of the physical world as a site of meaning. Jobs understood something that no technology company had understood before: that the store was the brand. That walking into an Apple Store was a religious experience, and that this was not a metaphor.

The stores are temples. The layout — open, white, minimal — is the architecture of a church without doctrine. The Genius Bar is the confessional booth where you bring your broken thing and a trained priest helps you restore it. The products are displayed like relics. Nothing is priced. You don't come in to shop. You come in to believe.

The Fifth Avenue cube — open 24 hours, glass, a literal beacon — is Apple's most honest building. It doesn't pretend to be a store. It's a lantern. The glass box you enter to descend underground to the space where the products live: you go down into the temple. You go below the city. The cube is Taurus's earthly beauty made sacred infrastructure.

Jobs called the four signature stores — Manhattan, Chicago, London, Tokyo — his four temples. He said this without irony. He was a Zen Buddhist. He understood that objects carry meaning, that space shapes consciousness, that beauty is not decorative. It is load-bearing.

05 Feature
The Body Becomes Interface

The Body Becomes
the Interface

The Cook era brought Apple into Virgo territory: health, the body, precision, the integration of biological data into daily life. Apple Watch and Apple Health represent the company's most intimate product category yet — and the most Virgoan.

⌚

April 24, 2015 · Taurus

Apple Watch

The first device Apple had made that required physical contact to work — you wear it, it touches you, it measures you. Taurus governs the body as a site of sensory experience, and the Watch arrived under that sign to claim the wrist. It counts your heartbeats. It knows when you've fallen. It suggests when to breathe.

This is Apple moving from the hand (iPhone) to the wrist to, eventually, the body as total interface. Virgo as destination: the health-monitoring, bio-tracking, precision-wellness future that Cook has been building toward since 2014.

🫀

September 17, 2014 · Virgo

Apple Health

Virgo rules health, the body's systems, the detail-oriented practices of maintenance and care. Apple Health launched under its own sign: September 17, Virgo. A centralised platform for every biometric the iPhone could collect — steps, sleep, heart rate, reproductive cycles, medication schedules.

Jobs died of a pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour — a disease whose early detection is now part of what Apple's health monitoring is built to catch. There is something almost unbearable about that arc: the company he built, operating under the sign that governs the precise bodily care he famously resisted, building tools to catch what killed him.

06 Feature
The Apple Shadow

The Cost of
Enchantment

The shadow of every mythology is the thing it cannot look at directly. Apple's mythology is built on beauty, simplicity, the democratisation of creative power. The shadow is the supply chain that manufactures that beauty — and the labour conditions, the locked ecosystems, the environmental cost of the annual upgrade cycle.

Foxconn. The suicides at the Zhengzhou factory in 2010 and 2011. The nets installed outside windows. Apple's response — audits, supplier responsibility reports, incremental improvements — reads like a company trying to discharge a debt it cannot fully acknowledge.

The Pisces shadow is denial — the refusal to see what disrupts the vision. Jobs' biographers describe a man who could hold two truths simultaneously: that the products were beautiful and that the conditions of their manufacture were not. The Pisces chart allowed him to operate inside that contradiction without apparent discomfort.

The Capricorn shadow is the shadow of ambition that cannot stop. Apple's planned obsolescence — the product cycle that renders last year's device functionally slow within eighteen months — is a Capricorn mechanism: the institution perpetuating itself through engineered scarcity, structured desire.

The Sagittarius shadow is the shadow of the myth that outgrows its truth. Think Different. The campaign aired in 1997 and showed Einstein, Gandhi, Picasso — the dissidents, the dreamers, the ones who changed history. Apple used their images to sell computers. The gap between the mythology and the product is where the shadow lives.

None of this makes the products less beautiful. None of it makes the mythology less powerful. The shadow is not the opposite of the light. It is the shape the light makes when something stands in it. Apple stands in its own light constantly. The shape it casts is the shadow.

A zine about corporate astrology is not a takedown. It is an attempt to see all of it — the brilliant, the flawed, the structurally necessary contradictions — through a different kind of lens. The sky doesn't judge. It just records.

"The shadow is not the opposite of the light. It is the shape the light makes when something stands in it. Apple stands in its own light constantly."
— Astrology Collide, Issue 01 · The Apple Shadow
