Spec. A-001
🌸 Critically Endangered

Dendrophylax lindenii

The ghost orchid survives without leaves, drawing sustenance through roots fused to a single host tree. It blooms perhaps once a decade — if conditions are precisely right.

Spec. A-017
🌿 Vulnerable

Welwitschia mirabilis

Two leaves. Only ever two. The Welwitschia grows them for two thousand years, splitting and fraying in the Namib wind into what looks like wreckage but is, in fact, survival perfected.

Spec. A-044
🌱 Endangered

Amorphophallus titanum

The corpse flower stores energy for up to ten years, then erupts for 48 hours in an inflorescence that can reach three metres — and smells, deliberately, of rotting flesh.

Spec. A-088
🌲 Critically Endangered

Wollemia nobilis

Discovered in 1994 in a canyon west of Sydney. Botanists thought it had been extinct for two million years. Only 100 trees exist in the wild, in a location kept secret by the government.

Spec. A-112
🌺 Near Threatened

Tacca chantrieri

The black bat flower produces the deepest black pigmentation found in any flowering plant, with whisker-like filaments extending up to 70 cm. It grows in a single narrow band of tropical forest floor.

Spec. A-203
🪴 Vulnerable

Rafflesia arnoldii

The world's largest individual flower, with no roots, stems, or leaves. It lives entirely inside the tissue of a host vine, invisible for years, before manifesting as a single enormous bloom.

🌿 Welwitschia mirabilis · Namib Desert 02

Specimen Study

Welwitschia:
A Living Fossil

Welwitschia mirabilis · Hook.f. · 1863

In 1859, the botanist Friedrich Welwitsch fell to his knees before a plant he had never seen in any taxonomy. He wept. He later wrote that he could not bring himself to collect a specimen — that disturbing it felt like desecration.

Welwitschia is the sole living species of its order. It has two leaves. Only ever two. Both grow continuously from the base, the tips weathering and fraying over centuries in the Namib wind. The oldest confirmed specimens are over 1,500 years old.

🌱 Amorphophallus titanum · Sumatra 03

Botany

The Corpse Flower's Decade of Patience

Amorphophallus titanum · Becc. · 1878

The titan arum doesn't flower on a schedule you can plan around. It gathers carbohydrates for up to a decade, building a corm that can weigh 90 kilograms, and when it finally blooms, the event lasts 48 hours. It heats its spathe to 36°C — human body temperature — to volatilise the compounds that smell like a rotting corpse.

This is not an accident. The heat propels the scent for hundreds of metres, attracting the carrion beetles that will carry pollen to another titan arum, if one happens to be blooming nearby. Timing, across a forest, with no roots connecting them.

🌫️ Cloud Forest · Manu, Peru · 3,400 m 04

Expedition Report

Cloud Forest:
28 Days at 3,400 m

Manu Biosphere Reserve · Peru · 2025

The cloud forest exists in a band of perpetual mist. Below, the Amazon basin. Above, the altiplano. Here, in the wet zone between 2,500 and 3,800 metres, plants evolved in isolation long enough to become entirely new forms.

Our team spent 28 days in a single 4-hectare plot. We documented 14 species new to science — among them a miniature orchid with a petal span of 2.1 mm, visible only under magnification, and a bromeliad that had evolved to eat insects rather than wait for nutrients to arrive through rain.

Cultivation Notes
5 Protocols

Growing the Uncultivable

Protocol I
💧 Humidity Control

Maintain 85–95% relative humidity for cloud forest epiphytes. Daily misting at dawn replicates condensation cycles.

Protocol II
🌡️ Thermal Rhythm

A 10°C night-to-day temperature drop triggers dormancy cycles critical for orchid bloom induction.

Protocol III
🪨 Substrate Composition

For desert specimens: 60% coarse pumice, 30% decomposed granite, 10% aged bark. Zero organic matter retained.

Protocol IV
🔬 Mycorrhizal Inoculation

Ghost orchid seeds require specific fungal partners. Collect substrate from beneath existing specimens and transfer directly.

Protocol V
📅 Patience as Method

Titan arum corms require 7–10 years before first bloom. There is no shortcut. Document and wait.
