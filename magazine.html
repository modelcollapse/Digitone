Corporations Are People.
So What's Their Sign?

When Mitt Romney said "corporations are people," something clicked for me. Not politically. Symbolically. I was working in branding at Wolff Olins at the time — surrounded daily by the language of identity, personality, belief systems, archetypes, desire, and myth. In branding, we already treat corporations like people. We give them voices, values, origin stories, shadow traits, enemies, promises, and moods. We build entire worlds around them — and then call it just business.

But corporations are not just businesses. They are symbolic bodies. They have birth dates, founders, myths, wounds, public personas, and private compulsions. A logo is a sigil. A keynote is a ritual. A flagship store is a temple. A founder story is a creation myth. A launch date is an initiation.

There is a line attributed to J.P. Morgan: "Millionaires don't use astrology. Billionaires do." The quote is difficult to verify — historians trace it to a book published decades after Morgan's death. But its persistence matters almost as much as its origin. Ronald Reagan's administration offered something less deniable: Nancy Reagan consulted astrologer Joan Quigley for seven years after the 1981 assassination attempt. According to former Chief of Staff Donald Regan, virtually every major move was cleared in advance — departure times for Air Force One, scheduling of summits with Gorbachev, timing of press conferences. Astrology was not at the fringe of that administration. It was near the machinery of state.

For the first issue of Astrology Collide, there is no better place to begin than Apple. Apple is a symbol before it is a stock. Its founding date gives us a chart. Its name gives us a symbol — the garden, the bite, the forbidden knowledge. Its stores give us temples. And here is a detail that never gets enough attention: before Apple existed, the twenty-year-old Jobs was at Atari writing equations for a horoscope software program called Astrochart. The man who would build devices that feel almost divinatory literally began his programming career building a divination system.

Apple did not simply sell us computers. It sold us a self-image — creative, chosen, intuitive, different. Which means Apple was never just a brand. It was always a chart waiting to be read. That tension is the whole story. And the chart is where we begin.

The Three Births

Three Births,
Three Charts

Most companies have one founding date. Apple has three — and each one tells a different story about what the company is. Not versions of the same story. Different stories. Which means Apple has always been plural. It has always been more than one thing at once.

♈

April 1, 1976

The Soul Chart

Aries · The Rebel Spark · Tarot: The Emperor

April Fool's Day — which is either accidental or the most on-brand founding date in tech history. Two young men in a California garage, a homemade circuit board, and a refusal to accept that computing belonged to corporations and institutions. Aries is the first sign of the zodiac. It does not ask permission. It acts, then adjusts.

In tarot, Aries corresponds to The Emperor — which seems counterintuitive for a company that styled itself as the rebel. But this is Aries' paradox: the rebel and the empire-builder share the same origin. The Emperor is structure born from will. He does not inherit territory. He declares it.

Jungian lens: The Hero. The Aries Apple is the Hero at the threshold — technology removed from the priesthood of experts and placed into human hands. Promethean myth, California edition. The shadow of the Hero arrives early too: the computer for the rest of us eventually became the ecosystem you cannot easily leave.

♑

January 3, 1977

The Body Chart

Capricorn · The Sealed Object · Tarot: The Devil

Nine months after the spark, Apple becomes a legal entity. The incorporation date gives us Capricorn — and suddenly everything about Apple's design philosophy makes sense from a different angle. Capricorn is the mountain. It is structure, form, discipline, and the slow deliberate climb toward enduring power. Apple is — above almost all else — a company obsessed with form. The edge. The weight. The surface. The invisible screw. The seam that appears to have no seam.

In tarot, Capricorn corresponds to The Devil — and The Devil in symbolic terms is not evil. It is attachment, desire, glamour, and the seduction of beautiful material things. Apple makes objects so beautiful that we develop relationships with them. We touch them hundreds of times a day. We sleep beside them. We mourn when they break.

Jungian lens: The Persona and the Shadow. Apple understands persona better than almost any company in history. To own an Apple device is to appear as a certain kind of person. But every Persona casts a Shadow — control dressed as simplicity, a sealed ecosystem, spiritual language of creativity wrapped around a vast machine of supply chain secrecy.

♐

December 12, 1980

The Market Persona

Sagittarius · The Gospel Goes Public · Tarot: Temperance

Apple went public on December 12, 1980 — the largest IPO in the United States since Ford in 1956, raising $100 million and creating more than forty instant millionaires. Individual investors in Massachusetts were barred from buying shares by state regulators who deemed them "too risky." A $10,000 investment on IPO day would be worth approximately $32.7 million in 2026.

In tarot, Sagittarius corresponds to Temperance — the alchemist who blends two substances into something new. Apple blends art and engineering, beauty and utility, individuality and mass production. These combinations should not work. Temperance makes the impossible blend possible.

Jungian lens: The Magician. Steve Jobs did not present products like a salesman. He presented them like revelations. "One more thing" was not a product cue. It was a ritual phrase. The Magician's shadow is manipulation: the ability to enchant is also the ability to distort.

The Big Three

Sun in Pisces, 6th House

Jobs' Pisces Sun placed in the 6th house of daily work is the foundational paradox of his character. Pisces is the visionary dreamer — boundless, imaginative, emotionally porous. But the 6th house grounds that vision in relentless daily craft, perfectionist routine, and service through labor. This placement produces the rare type who can access pure, boundary-less creative vision and then transmute it into disciplined, production-grade work. His famous fourteen-hour work days were not workaholic dysfunction. They were a 6th house Pisces expressing its nature: vision made into daily habit.

Moon in Aries, 7th House

The Aries Moon is fast, fiery, impatient, and competitive. In the 7th house of partnerships, this explains two central biographical facts: Jobs' notorious interpersonal intensity — his management by confrontation — and his pattern of deeply formative creative partnerships (Wozniak, Jony Ive, John Lasseter). He genuinely believed challenge and even cruelty produced better work. Partners were not collaborators to be protected. They were combatants to be pushed past their limits.

Virgo Rising

The Ascendant defines the outward persona. Virgo rising gives Jobs his exterior of precision, critique, and discriminating perfectionism. The hundreds of designs rejected, the products cancelled weeks before launch, the obsessive attention to parts of the product that customers would never see. His design mantra — "Design is not just what it looks like and feels like. Design is how it works" — is a perfect verbal expression of Virgo rising: the outer appearance must be an accurate reflection of inner function, not a facade over emptiness.

Mercury Retrograde in Aquarius

The Retrograde Genius

Mercury rules both his Ascendant (Virgo) and his Midheaven (Gemini). A single planet is the master of both his public face and his career identity. Mercury in Aquarius places his thinking in the sign of technology, the future, and radical departure from convention. In the 5th house of creativity, this Mercury becomes exuberant in sharing ideas — a Jobs keynote was not a product announcement. It was a theatrical event.

The retrograde status appears at first as a weakness. But when a planet is retrograde, it is actually at its closest approach to Earth — brighter, more intense, not weaker. Jobs' retrograde Mercury produces not a communicator who speaks first but one who incubates deeply and then communicates with unusual force. The long development cycles. The refusal to ship before perfection.

At 14° Aquarius, Mercury creates a critical geometric link with Apple's corporate chart: Apple's founding North Node sits at 14° Scorpio, forming a square to Jobs' natal Mercury. This is the astrological signature of their karmic interlock.

The Founders as Archetypes

Four Men, Four Archetypes

Every company at its origin is a cast of characters playing roles that the mythology later simplifies. Apple had four founders. Most people remember one. But each brought something the others couldn't — and each embodies a different relationship to the work of building something new.

🌊

The Magician · Tarot I

Steve Jobs

Sun Pisces · Moon Aries · Mercury Aquarius Rx

The Pisces Sun gives Jobs his peculiar quality — the ability to see what doesn't exist yet and treat it as already real. Pisces is the final sign, the sign that dissolves boundaries, the sign of the visionary who cannot always distinguish between the world as it is and the world as it could be. In Jobs, this was not a flaw. It was the mechanism.

His Aries Moon is the urgency beneath the vision — the emotional need for initiation, for motion, for the constant arrival of the new. The famous reality distortion field is not a trick. It is the natural output of a Pisces Sun convinced of what it sees, operating through an Aries Moon that cannot tolerate stillness.

Mercury retrograde in Aquarius: Jobs' mind was unconventional even by unconventional standards. He thought backwards from a future most people couldn't picture. Mercury Rx suggests a processing style that internalises before it speaks — which explains both his silences and his pronouncements.

⚡

The Inventor · Tarot XVII

Steve Wozniak

Sun Aquarius · The Engineer of the Impossible

Wozniak is the Aquarian ideal made flesh: the technologist as humanitarian, the inventor who built the Apple I as a gift for friends and was surprised when Jobs saw a product in it. The Apple I and Apple II were Wozniak's machines — technically brilliant, built for joy, almost entirely uninterested in commerce.

Aquarius is the sign of the future arriving early. Wozniak arrived early. He was doing in 1975 what the industry would spend the next decade learning to do. He didn't need the mythology that Jobs built around the work — the work was its own mythology to him.

The partnership of Jobs and Wozniak is an Aries/Aquarius alliance: fire and air, the visionary and the inventor, the salesman and the engineer. Neither could have built what they built without the other, and both knew it, and that knowledge didn't always make things easy.

🚶

The One Who Walked Away · Tarot 0

Ronald Wayne

The Third Founder · Sold His Stake for $800

Ronald Wayne is the third founder nobody talks about. He drew the original Apple logo — Newton under the apple tree — and wrote the first partnership agreement. Twelve days after signing, he sold his 10% stake back to Jobs and Wozniak for $800. At Apple's 2023 valuation, that stake would have been worth approximately $300 billion.

Wayne later said he had no regrets. He had his reasons — he was older, had other debts, and assessed the risk differently than two men in their twenties with nothing to lose. His is the archetype of the Fool at the beginning of the tarot: the one who stands at the edge, sees the cliff, and steps back.

The Wayne story is the shadow story of every founding. Not every character who appears at the origin survives to the mythology. His exit is as much a part of Apple's chart as any planet.

♍

The Administrator · Tarot VIII

Tim Cook

Sun Scorpio · The Virgo Era Made Flesh

Jobs handed the company to Cook on August 24, 2011 — a Virgo date, a Virgo transfer. Cook is the archetype of Strength, the tarot card of inner mastery, of power held rather than performed. Where Jobs operated from Pisces dissolution and Aries fire, Cook operates from precision, process, and supply chain as art form.

The Cook era is marked by Apple's move from products as mythology to products as infrastructure. The iPhone under Cook became the most profitable product in history. Under Cook, Apple crossed $1 trillion (August 2, 2018) and $3 trillion (January 2022). The sky on August 2, 2018 showed Uranus at 2° Taurus — nearly conjunct Jupiter at 1° Taurus from Apple's founding date.

Scorpio Sun, Virgo transition date, trillion-dollar scale. The administrator who made the myth into infrastructure.

04 Feature
The Spiritual Architecture

Before Apple,
There Was India

Before there was an Apple, there was an India journey. Before there was an iPhone, there was a Zen monk. Before there was a product launch, there was a horoscope program. The spiritual biography of Steve Jobs is not footnote material. It is the root system of everything Apple made.

India · 1974

The Journey That Changed Everything

In the summer of 1974, Jobs — then nineteen, a Reed College dropout working at Atari — left for India with his college friend Daniel Kottke to seek spiritual guidance. He traveled to the Kainchi Ashram of Neem Karoli Baba, a Hindu guru whose other devotees included Harvard professor Richard Alpert, later known as Ram Dass. He found the guru dead — Neem Karoli Baba had died in September 1973, months before Jobs arrived. Jobs and Kottke spent seven months traveling through the Indian countryside instead.

He returned to California with a shaved head, in traditional Indian clothing, and with a philosophy that would shape everything Apple made: minimalism, non-verbal knowing, the primacy of feel over specification. Jobs described it directly: "The people in the Indian countryside don't use their intellect like we do, they use their intuition instead, and their intuition is far more developed than in the rest of the world. Intuition is a very powerful thing, more powerful than intellect, in my opinion. That's had a big impact on my work."

Zen Buddhism and Design

The Monk, the Minimalism

Jobs studied Zen with Kobun Chino Otogawa at the Haiku Zen Center in Los Altos beginning in the mid-1970s. Kobun was later the Zen monk who married Jobs and Laurene Powell in 1991. The concrete design influence of Zen on Apple products is documented and acknowledged.

Mu — emptiness, negative space, the meaningful absence of things — is directly reflected in Apple's refusal to include unnecessary buttons, ports, or visual elements. The removal of the headphone jack. The single button. The trackpad with no buttons. Mu made physical. Robert Thurman of Columbia University's Buddhist studies program described Jobs not as a practicing Buddhist but as someone who genuinely absorbed Zen's "compelling vision" — particularly the concept of suchness: things as they are, without conceptual overlay. That is the Apple unboxing experience. No instruction manual. Just the object, presented as itself.

Atari · 1975

The First Program Was a Horoscope

Before Apple existed, the twenty-year-old Jobs was at Atari writing mathematical equations for a horoscope software program called Astrochart. A January 2026 tech history piece on Adafruit documented the recreation of this program, confirming Jobs was involved in building astrology software in 1975 — the year before he co-founded Apple.

The man who built devices that feel divinatory in their ability to anticipate user desire literally began his programming career building a divination system. The first software Jobs worked on was a horoscope. His last major project was Apple Park — a building whose circular design, reflecting pools, and precise orientation suggest cosmological intent. The arc from Astrochart to Apple Park is, at minimum, symbolically coherent.

Numerology

The Number 28

Jobs' life path number is 28 — the sum 2+4+2+1+9+5+5 equals 28, a perfect number in mathematics, equal to the sum of its divisors (1+2+4+7+14 = 28). Apple was founded on April 1, 1976: 4+1+1+9+7+6 also equals 28. Whether deliberate or synchronistic, the recurrence of 28 as a root number across Jobs' birth date and Apple's founding date is part of the cosmic signature of the Jobs-Apple phenomenon. The number 28 corresponds to one lunar cycle — the moon returns to its starting position every 28 days.

The Product Rituals

Products as Rituals

Every Apple product launch happens on a specific date, in a specific sky. Over forty years, a pattern emerges: certain signs appear at pivotal moments, Jupiter and Saturn mark the transitions, and the launches that changed everything tend to cluster around cardinal points and sign-change boundaries.

Jan 22–24, 1984

☉ Aquarius

The Macintosh

Aquarius · Rebellion Against the Machine

The "1984" ad airs January 22nd, two days before the launch — staging Apple as liberator: a lone runner, a hypnotized mass audience, a hammer thrown at centralized control. The Mac arrives under Aquarius and says the machine does not have to belong to the institution. It can belong to the person. Forty years later, that promise still drives Apple's marketing language.

Nov 10, 1997

☉ Scorpio

The Online Apple Store

Scorpio · Revenge Through Direct Commerce

Two months after Jobs returned as interim CEO, Apple launched its online store under Scorpio — the sign of control, hidden systems, and the return of power from those who took it. No more middlemen. This move predates the retail stores by four years. It is the first act of a Scorpio strategy that would become the App Store, Apple Music, Apple TV+, and Apple Pay — a services empire built on the principle that Apple owns the relationship end to end.

Oct 23, 2001

☉ Scorpio

The iPod

Scorpio · The Private Music Vault

A thousand songs in your pocket. What Jobs was actually describing was a private emotional vault: memory, longing, taste, identity, and the entire soundtrack of a self compressed into a small white object. The white earbuds signaled precisely Scorpionic: I am connected to my own interior world. Do not interrupt. The iPod turned music from a shared, public experience into a private, portable one. It made the inner life portable.

Apr 28, 2003

☉ Taurus

The iTunes Store

Taurus · Music Becomes an Object Again

The iTunes Music Store arrives under Taurus — the sign of material value, ownership, and the tangible. In the era of Napster, music had become unstable: free, invisible, uncontained, impossible to own. Apple's answer was to restore materiality. Ninety-nine cents. One song. Yours. Taurus restores object status to something that had dissolved into pure digital ether. Apple did not merely sell songs. It rebuilt the relationship between music and possession.

Jan 9 / Jun 29, 2007

♑ then ♋

The iPhone

Capricorn then Cancer · Authority, Then Intimacy

The iPhone has two births. The announcement on January 9 falls under Capricorn — the controlled reveal of a definitive object that reorganizes the hierarchy of an entire industry. Jobs introduces it as three devices in one, then says: Apple is introducing one device. The room erupts. But the iPhone releases five months later on June 29, under Cancer. And Cancer is home, attachment, emotional memory, intimacy, the container we live inside. Capricorn made it authoritative. Cancer made it indispensable.

Apr 3, 2010

☉ Aries

The iPad

Aries · The Cycle Completes

The iPad launches under Aries — closing a circle that opened with Apple's founding in April 1976. Critics called it a large iPhone. They missed the point. The iPad was Apple's declaration that the computer interface itself needed to be rebuilt from first principles — not adapted, not miniaturized, but reimagined. That is Aries: ignition, rupture, the courage to begin before the argument is settled. The iPad also arrives near Jupiter conjunct Jobs' natal Sun — the last major product he introduces while healthy.

Feb 2, 2024

☉ Aquarius

Apple Vision Pro

Aquarius Again · Reality Becomes Interface

Vision Pro releases under Aquarius — the same sign as the Macintosh, forty years later. The echo is not subtle. Aquarius rules future systems, altered social architecture, and the redesign of perception through invention. The Vision Pro is not a screen. It is an attempt to place the Apple interface into the field of vision itself. Apple's oldest question — where does the tool end and the self begin — arrives here at its most extreme form.

The Store as Temple
🏛️ Fifth Avenue · New York · 2006

Retail as Sacred Architecture

Four Temples
and a Glass Cube

The first Apple Store opened May 19, 2001 — Taurus season, the sign of beautiful objects, of value made tangible, of the physical world as a site of meaning. Jobs understood something that no technology company had understood before: that the store was the brand. That walking into an Apple Store was a religious experience, and that this was not a metaphor.

The stores are temples. The layout — open, white, minimal — is the architecture of a church without doctrine. The Genius Bar is the confessional booth where you bring your broken thing and a trained priest helps you restore it. The products are displayed like relics. Nothing is priced. You don't come in to shop. You come in to believe.

The Fifth Avenue cube — open 24 hours, glass, a literal beacon — is Apple's most honest building. It doesn't pretend to be a store. It's a lantern. The glass box you enter to descend underground to the space where the products live: you go down into the temple. You go below the city. The cube is Taurus's earthly beauty made sacred infrastructure.

Jobs called the four signature stores — Manhattan, Chicago, London, Tokyo — his four temples. He said this without irony. He was a Zen Buddhist. He understood that objects carry meaning, that space shapes consciousness, that beauty is not decorative. It is load-bearing.

The Body Becomes
the Interface

The Cook era brought Apple into Virgo territory: health, the body, precision, the integration of biological data into daily life. Apple Watch and Apple Health represent the company's most intimate product category yet — and the most Virgoan.

⌚

April 24, 2015 · Taurus

Apple Watch

The first device Apple had made that required physical contact to work — you wear it, it touches you, it measures you. Taurus governs the body as a site of sensory experience, and the Watch arrived under that sign to claim the wrist. It counts your heartbeats. It knows when you've fallen. It suggests when to breathe.

This is Apple moving from the hand (iPhone) to the wrist to, eventually, the body as total interface. Virgo as destination: the health-monitoring, bio-tracking, precision-wellness future that Cook has been building toward since 2014.

🫀

September 17, 2014 · Virgo

Apple Health

Virgo rules health, the body's systems, the detail-oriented practices of maintenance and care. Apple Health launched under its own sign: September 17, Virgo. A centralised platform for every biometric the iPhone could collect — steps, sleep, heart rate, reproductive cycles, medication schedules.

Jobs died of a pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour — a disease whose early detection is now part of what Apple's health monitoring is built to catch. There is something almost unbearable about that arc: the company he built, operating under the sign that governs the precise bodily care he famously resisted, building tools to catch what killed him.

The Apple Corporate Chart

Cosmic Coordinates

The founding chart, the $1 trillion alignment, and the evidence that makes astrological analysts regard Apple as the best corporate case study in the modern record.

The Founding Chart · April 1, 1976

Jupiter at 1° Taurus,
North Node at 14° Scorpio

On the day the Apple Computer Company partnership was signed: Jupiter at 1° Taurus — the planet of abundance sitting on the money and business sign at its most foundational degree. North Node at 14° Scorpio — the karmic North Node in the sign of high finance, transformation, and corporate power. South Node at 14° Taurus — creating a Taurus-Scorpio financial axis across the chart. Ron Wayne typed the partnership document on that date and sold his 10% stake for $800 days later.

August 2, 2018

The $1 Trillion Alignment

The day Apple became the first publicly traded company worth $1 trillion, two things happened simultaneously in the sky:

Transiting Uranus reached 2° Taurus — landing within one degree of Apple's natal Jupiter at 1° Taurus. Uranus takes 84 years to complete one orbit. Jung's concept of the Shadow is not about evil. It is about what we cannot see in ourselves because we are too identified with the opposite quality. The Shadow is the cost of the gift. The underside of the strength.

Simplicity→ Control
Beauty→ Perfectionism as cruelty
Ecosystem→ Enclosure
Privacy→ Opacity
Devotion→ Dependency
Design→ Invisible labor

Apple's Capricorn incorporation chart gave it The Devil — and The Devil has been present in every product Apple has ever made. Not as evil. As attachment. The beautiful object that you desire so completely that it begins to organize your life around itself. The cleaner the object, the more hidden the machinery behind it. The smoother the surface, the less visible the hands that polished it.

The most powerful form of control is the kind that feels like freedom. You are not trapped. You just don't want to leave. Apple has always understood this. The sealed ecosystem. The locked repair path. The spiritual language of creativity wrapped around a vast machine of supply chain secrecy, labor extraction, and capital.

The invisible labor shadow is perhaps the hardest to sit with, because it requires holding Apple's beautiful objects alongside the conditions of their manufacture — the supply chain that Apple manages with extraordinary Scorpionic control, the factories that produce the devices, the extraction of materials, the working conditions of the people whose hands make what our hands hold. The object is clean. The system behind it is not. That gap is where Apple's deepest shadow lives.

Jung's Shadow work is not about condemning the Shadow. It is about seeing it clearly enough that it stops operating unconsciously. The goal is not to stop using Apple products. The goal is to understand what the relationship actually is — what we project onto the devices, what needs they address, what parts of ourselves we have outsourced to them.

The shadow is not the opposite of the light. It is the shape the light makes when something stands in it. Apple stands in its own light constantly. What part of ourselves did we place inside the device? And do we know how to get it back?
